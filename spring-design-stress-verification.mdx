A spring can give exactly the right force on the test bench and still break after a few months in the machine. The wire was stressed too close to its limit, and each cycle did a little damage until a crack ran through a coil. In this lesson you size a spring from its loads, check the wire stress against a static limit and a fatigue limit, and then build the 3D model in CadQuery, a Python library that makes solid models from code. #SpringDesign #StressAnalysis #MechanicalEngineering

## Spring Types and Their Applications

<InArticleAd />

A helical spring is a wire wound into coils. The load it resists decides which stress the wire carries.

```text
  COMPRESSION          EXTENSION            TORSION
  (resists push)       (resists pull)       (resists twist)

     ↓ F                  ↑ F                   ↻ M
   ┌─────┐            ┌───────┐            ┌───────┐
   │  O  │             │ hook  │            │ leg   │
   │  O  │ coils       │ O O O │ coils      │ O O O │ coils
   │  O  │ apart       │ O O O │ touch      │ O O O │ touch
   │  O  │             │ hook  │            │  leg  │
   └─────┘            └───────┘            └───────┘
   wire: shear         wire: shear          wire: bending
```

  <TabItem label="Compression Springs">

The most common type. The coils are wound with a gap and close up under load. Examples: valve springs, battery contacts, push buttons.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Extension Springs">

</TabItem>

Two legs apply a turning load (torque). The name misleads: the wire is bent, not twisted. Examples: clothes pegs, binder clips, mouse traps.

## Compression Spring Design Equations

<InArticleAd />


A compression spring looks squeezed, but its wire is twisted. The force $F$ acts along the axis, and the wire sits half a coil diameter $D$ away from it. A force at a distance is a torque, so the wire carries the torque $T = F \cdot D / 2$, as a bolt is twisted by a hand on the end of a wrench.

```text
        F │   force on the spring axis
          ▼
   ┌──────┼──────┐
   │ O    ┊    O │   O = wire, cut through
   │ O    ┊    O │
   └──────┼──────┘   ├──────┤ arm = D/2
          ▲          torque in the wire = F × D/2
        F │
```

The equations hold for round wire and a load along the axis.

### Spring Rate

The spring rate $k$ is the force needed for each millimetre of deflection:

$$
k = \frac{F}{\delta} = \frac{G d^4}{8 D^3 N_a}
$$

where:
- $k$ = spring rate (N/mm), $F$ = force (N), $\delta$ = deflection (mm)
- $G$ = shear modulus of the wire (MPa), its stiffness against twisting
- $d$ = wire diameter (mm)
- $D$ = mean coil diameter (mm), measured between wire centers
- $N_a$ = number of active coils, those free to deflect

The rate grows with $d^4$ and falls with $D^3$: wire 20% thinner gives only 41% of the rate.

### Spring Index

The spring index $C$ says how tightly the wire is curved. It has no unit:

$$
C = \frac{D}{d}
$$

Keep $4 \leq C \leq 12$. Below 4 the wire is bent so sharply that it can crack in coiling, and the stress inside the coil rises steeply. Above 12 the spring is loose: it tangles and buckles.

### Deflection and Free Length

$$
\delta = \frac{F}{k} = \frac{8 F D^3 N_a}{G d^4}
$$

The free length $L_f$ is the length with no load. It holds the wire itself, the largest deflection, and a reserve so the coils never touch in service:

$$
L_f = L_s + \delta_{\text{max}} + \delta_{\text{clash}}
$$

where:
- $L_s = (N_a + 2) \cdot d$ is the solid height (mm), the length when all coils touch. Squared-and-ground ends, closed and ground flat so the spring stands straight, add 2 coils that do not deflect.
- $\delta_{\text{clash}}$ is the reserve (mm), 10 to 15% of the working deflection

### Pitch and Coil Spacing

$$
p = \frac{L_f - 2d}{N_a}
$$

where $p$ is the pitch (mm), the distance along the axis from one active coil to the next.

### Wire Diameter Selection from Standard Gauges

Wire is sold in standard diameters, so round a calculated diameter up to the next size. Thin wire is stronger than thick wire: the curve fit $S_{ut} = A / d^m$ in the code gives about 2,000 MPa at 2 mm.

```python
# Standard music wire diameters (mm): partial table
MUSIC_WIRE_DIAMETERS = [
    0.10, 0.12, 0.14, 0.16, 0.18, 0.20, 0.22, 0.25,
    0.28, 0.30, 0.35, 0.40, 0.45, 0.50, 0.55, 0.60,
    0.65, 0.70, 0.80, 0.90, 1.00, 1.10, 1.20, 1.40,
    1.60, 1.80, 2.00, 2.20, 2.50, 2.80, 3.00, 3.50,
    4.00, 4.50, 5.00, 5.50, 6.00, 6.50, 7.00, 8.00,
    9.00, 10.00, 11.00, 12.00, 13.00, 14.00, 15.00, 16.00,
]

# Approximate tensile strength of music wire (Sut in MPa)
# Using the empirical formula: Sut = A / d^m
# For music wire (ASTM A228): A = 2211 MPa·mm^m, m = 0.145
def music_wire_tensile_strength(d_mm: float) -> float:
    """Approximate ultimate tensile strength of music wire.

    Based on curve fit to ASTM A228 data.
    Args:
        d_mm: Wire diameter in mm
    Returns:
        Ultimate tensile strength in MPa
    """
    A = 2211.0  # MPa·mm^m
    m = 0.145
    return A / (d_mm ** m)

def select_wire_diameter(d_required: float) -> float:
    """Select the nearest standard wire diameter >= required."""
    for d_std in MUSIC_WIRE_DIAMETERS:
        if d_std >= d_required:
            return d_std
    raise ValueError(
        f"Required diameter {d_required} mm exceeds "
        f"maximum standard size {MUSIC_WIRE_DIAMETERS[-1]} mm"
    )
```

## Stress Analysis: Wahl Correction Factor

<InArticleAd />


Twisting a straight round bar with the torque $F D / 2$ gives the shear stress $\tau = 8FD / (\pi d^3)$ at its surface, in MPa for N and mm. Shear stress is the stress of layers sliding over each other. For a coiled wire this reads too low, for two reasons:

1. Curvature. The inside of the coil is shorter than the outside, so less material takes the same twist there.
2. Direct shear. The force $F$ also tries to cut straight across the wire, which adds stress on the inside of the coil.

The Wahl correction factor $K_w$ covers both. It depends only on the spring index $C$:

$$
K_w = \frac{4C - 1}{4C - 4} + \frac{0.615}{C}
$$

It is 1.40 at $C = 4$, 1.18 at $C = 8$ and 1.12 at $C = 12$. The highest shear stress, on the inside of the coil, is:

$$
\tau_{\max} = K_w \cdot \frac{8 F D}{\pi d^3}
$$

It must stay below an allowable value:

$$
\tau_{\max} \leq \tau_{\text{allow}} = \frac{S_{sy}}{n_s}
$$

where $S_{sy}$ is the shear yield strength (MPa), at which the wire takes a permanent set, and $n_s$ is the safety factor, 1.2 to 1.5 for a steady load. For music wire $S_{sy}$ is about $0.45 \cdot S_{ut}$, or $0.60$ to $0.70 \cdot S_{ut}$ after set removal.

The code's example, 50 N with $D = 20$ mm and $d = 2$ mm, prints $K_w = 1.145$ and 364.4 MPa.

```python
import numpy as np

def wahl_factor(C: float) -> float:
    """Compute the Wahl correction factor.

    Args:
        C: Spring index (D/d), must be >= 3

    Returns:
        Wahl factor K_w
    """
    if C < 3:
        raise ValueError(f"Spring index C={C} is too low (min 3)")
    return (4*C - 1) / (4*C - 4) + 0.615 / C

def max_shear_stress(F: float, D: float, d: float) -> float:
    """Compute maximum shear stress with Wahl correction.

    Args:
        F: Applied force (N)
        D: Mean coil diameter (mm)
        d: Wire diameter (mm)

    Returns:
        Maximum shear stress (MPa)
    """
    C = D / d
    Kw = wahl_factor(C)
    tau = Kw * 8 * F * D / (np.pi * d**3)
    return tau

# Example: 50 N force, 20mm mean coil diameter, 2mm wire
tau = max_shear_stress(F=50, D=20, d=2.0)
print(f"Wahl factor: {wahl_factor(20/2):.3f}")
print(f"Max shear stress: {tau:.1f} MPa")
```

## Fatigue Analysis: Goodman Diagram

<InArticleAd />


A paper clip bent once does not break. Bent back and forth, it breaks under a small load. This is fatigue: a repeated stress can start a crack without ever reaching the yield strength. A valve spring cycles millions of times, so it is designed for infinite life, in practice more than $10^6$ to $10^7$ cycles.

### Alternating and Mean Stress

Fatigue damage depends on how far the stress swings and on the level it swings around. Split the load, which cycles between $F_{\min}$ and $F_{\max}$, into an alternating part $F_a$ and a mean part $F_m$ (N):

$$
F_a = \frac{F_{\max} - F_{\min}}{2}, \quad F_m = \frac{F_{\max} + F_{\min}}{2}
$$

Each gives a shear stress by the same formula as before:

$$
\tau_a = K_w \cdot \frac{8 F_a D}{\pi d^3}, \quad \tau_m = K_w \cdot \frac{8 F_m D}{\pi d^3}
$$

### Goodman Criterion for Infinite Life

In the Goodman rule the swing and the mean each use a share of what the material can take, and the two shares together must stay below 1:

$$
\frac{\tau_a}{S_{se}} + \frac{\tau_m}{S_{su}} \leq \frac{1}{n_f}
$$

where:
- $S_{se}$ = endurance limit in shear (MPa), the swing the wire survives without limit at zero mean. About $0.30 \cdot S_{ut}$, or $0.40 \cdot S_{ut}$ after shot peening, which hammers the surface with small steel balls.
- $S_{su}$ = ultimate shear strength (MPa), about $0.67 \cdot S_{ut}$
- $n_f$ = fatigue safety factor, usually 1.3 to 2.0

These factors of $S_{ut}$ are typical values. For a spring that matters, use the wire maker's fatigue data.

The example in the code, $D = 18$ mm and $d = 2$ mm cycling between 20 N and 80 N, gives $\tau_a = 200$ MPa, $\tau_m = 333$ MPa and $n_f = 1.72$, above the required 1.5.

```python
def goodman_fatigue_check(
    F_min: float, F_max: float,
    D: float, d: float,
    S_ut: float,
    shot_peened: bool = False,
    n_f_required: float = 1.5
) -> dict:
    """Perform Goodman fatigue analysis on a helical spring.

    Args:
        F_min: Minimum cyclic force (N)
        F_max: Maximum cyclic force (N)
        D: Mean coil diameter (mm)
        d: Wire diameter (mm)
        S_ut: Ultimate tensile strength of wire (MPa)
        shot_peened: Whether the spring is shot-peened
        n_f_required: Required fatigue safety factor

    Returns:
        Dictionary with stress values and safety factor
    """
    C = D / d
    Kw = wahl_factor(C)

    # Alternating and mean forces
    F_a = (F_max - F_min) / 2
    F_m = (F_max + F_min) / 2

    # Corrected alternating and mean shear stresses
    tau_a = Kw * 8 * F_a * D / (np.pi * d**3)
    tau_m = Kw * 8 * F_m * D / (np.pi * d**3)

    # Material properties
    S_su = 0.67 * S_ut    # Ultimate shear strength
    if shot_peened:
        S_se = 0.40 * S_ut  # Endurance limit (peened)
    else:
        S_se = 0.30 * S_ut  # Endurance limit (unpeened)

    # Goodman safety factor
    # tau_a/S_se + tau_m/S_su = 1/n_f
    # Solving for n_f:
    goodman_sum = tau_a / S_se + tau_m / S_su
    if goodman_sum > 0:
        n_f = 1.0 / goodman_sum
    else:
        n_f = float('inf')

    return {
        'F_a': F_a,
        'F_m': F_m,
        'tau_a_MPa': tau_a,
        'tau_m_MPa': tau_m,
        'S_se_MPa': S_se,
        'S_su_MPa': S_su,
        'Kw': Kw,
        'C': C,
        'n_f': n_f,
        'infinite_life': n_f >= n_f_required,
    }

# Example: spring cycling between 20N and 80N
result = goodman_fatigue_check(
    F_min=20, F_max=80,
    D=18, d=2.0,
    S_ut=music_wire_tensile_strength(2.0),
    shot_peened=False,
    n_f_required=1.5
)

for key, val in result.items():
    if isinstance(val, float):
        print(f"  {key}: {val:.3f}")
    else:
        print(f"  {key}: {val}")
```

### Goodman Diagram Visualization

The diagram plots alternating stress against mean stress. The Goodman line runs from $S_{se}$ on the vertical axis to $S_{su}$ on the horizontal axis, and a spring is safe when its point lies below the line.

```python
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

def plot_goodman_diagram(result: dict, title: str = "Goodman Diagram"):
    """Plot the Goodman diagram showing the operating point
    relative to the failure boundary.

    Args:
        result: Output from goodman_fatigue_check()
    """
    S_se = result['S_se_MPa']
    S_su = result['S_su_MPa']
    tau_a = result['tau_a_MPa']
    tau_m = result['tau_m_MPa']

    fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 1, figsize=(8, 6))

    # Goodman line (failure boundary)
    tau_m_line = np.linspace(0, S_su, 100)
    tau_a_line = S_se * (1 - tau_m_line / S_su)
    ax.plot(tau_m_line, tau_a_line, 'r-', linewidth=2,
            label='Goodman line (failure)')

    # Yield line: tau_a + tau_m = S_sy
    S_sy = 0.45 * (S_se / 0.30)  # Back-calculate S_ut
    tau_a_yield = np.linspace(0, S_sy, 100)
    tau_m_yield = S_sy - tau_a_yield
    ax.plot(tau_m_yield, tau_a_yield, 'b--', linewidth=1.5,
            label='Yield line')

    # Safe region (shaded)
    ax.fill_between(tau_m_line, 0, tau_a_line,
                     alpha=0.1, color='green', label='Safe region')

    # Operating point
    ax.plot(tau_m, tau_a, 'ko', markersize=10, zorder=5)
    ax.annotate(
        f'Operating point\n'
        f'n_f = {result["n_f"]:.2f}',
        xy=(tau_m, tau_a),
        xytext=(tau_m + S_su*0.05, tau_a + S_se*0.1),
        fontsize=10,
        arrowprops=dict(arrowstyle='->', color='black'),
        bbox=dict(boxstyle='round,pad=0.3', facecolor='yellow',
                  alpha=0.8),
    )

    # Load line from origin through operating point
    if tau_m > 0:
        slope = tau_a / tau_m
        tau_m_load = np.linspace(0, S_su, 100)
        tau_a_load = slope * tau_m_load
        mask = tau_a_load <= S_se * 1.1
        ax.plot(tau_m_load[mask], tau_a_load[mask], 'g:',
                linewidth=1, label='Load line')

    ax.set_xlabel('Mean Shear Stress τ_m (MPa)', fontsize=12)
    ax.set_ylabel('Alternating Shear Stress τ_a (MPa)', fontsize=12)
    ax.set_title(title, fontsize=14)
    ax.legend(loc='upper right')
    ax.set_xlim(0, S_su * 1.1)
    ax.set_ylim(0, S_se * 1.1)
    ax.grid(True, alpha=0.3)

    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.savefig("goodman_diagram.png", dpi=150)
    plt.show()
```

## CadQuery Spring Geometry Generation

<InArticleAd />

All three functions compute points along the center line of the wire, join them with a smooth curve (a spline), and sweep a circle of the wire diameter along it, like paste pressed from a tube. The model shows shape and fit only. The stress checks come from the formulas above.

Look for the two flat end coils of the compression spring, the hooks of the extension spring, and the legs of the torsion spring at the angle you asked for.

```python
import cadquery as cq
import numpy as np

def sweep_round_wire(points, d: float) -> cq.Workplane:
    """Sweep a round wire of diameter d along a smooth path.

    The circle is drawn at the first point of the path, on a plane
    at a right angle to the direction of the path at that point.
    """
    path = cq.Workplane("XY").spline(points)
    start = cq.Vector(*points[0])
    tangent = path.val().tangentAt(0)
    wire_profile = (
        cq.Workplane(cq.Plane(origin=start, normal=tangent))
        .circle(d / 2)
    )
    return wire_profile.sweep(path, isFrenet=True)

def compression_spring(
    d: float,          # Wire diameter (mm)
    D: float,          # Mean coil diameter (mm)
    N_a: float,        # Number of active coils
    L_f: float,        # Free length (mm)
    end_type: str = "squared_ground"
) -> cq.Workplane:
    """Generate a 3D compression spring using helix + sweep.

    Args:
        d: Wire diameter (mm)
        D: Mean coil diameter (mm)
        N_a: Number of active coils
        L_f: Free length (mm)
        end_type: "squared_ground" or "plain"

    Returns:
        CadQuery solid of the spring
    """
    R = D / 2  # Mean coil radius

    # Calculate pitch for active coils
    if end_type == "squared_ground":
        # Squared-and-ground ends: 1 dead coil each end
        active_length = L_f - 2 * d
        pitch = active_length / N_a
        total_coils = N_a + 2
    else:
        pitch = L_f / N_a
        total_coils = N_a

    # Generate helix as a series of points
    points_per_coil = 16  # Angular resolution (the spline smooths between points)
    total_points = int(total_coils * points_per_coil)

    helix_points = []
    for i in range(total_points + 1):
        t = i / points_per_coil  # t in coil numbers

        angle = 2 * np.pi * (i / points_per_coil)

        # Axial position with end transitions
        if end_type == "squared_ground":
            if t < 1.0:
                # First dead coil: transition from zero pitch
                z = d * (t / 1.0)  # Linear ramp to pitch
            elif t > total_coils - 1.0:
                # Last dead coil: transition back to zero pitch
                t_end = t - (total_coils - 1.0)
                z_start = d + (N_a) * pitch
                z = z_start + d * (t_end / 1.0)  # Linear ramp over the last coil
            else:
                # Active coils: constant pitch
                z = d + (t - 1.0) * pitch
        else:
            z = t * pitch

        x = R * np.cos(angle)
        y = R * np.sin(angle)
        helix_points.append((x, y, z))

    # Create the helix path as a spline and sweep the circular
    # wire cross-section along it, starting normal to the helix
    spring = sweep_round_wire(helix_points, d)

    return spring

# Design a compression spring
spring = compression_spring(
    d=2.0,       # 2mm wire diameter
    D=16.0,      # 16mm mean coil diameter
    N_a=8,       # 8 active coils
    L_f=45.0,    # 45mm free length
    end_type="squared_ground"
)

# Export
cq.exporters.export(spring, "compression_spring.step")
cq.exporters.export(spring, "compression_spring.stl")
```

```python
import cadquery as cq
import numpy as np

def extension_spring(
    d: float,          # Wire diameter (mm)
    D: float,          # Mean coil diameter (mm)
    N_a: float,        # Number of active coils (body)
    hook_length: float = None,  # Hook straight length (mm)
) -> cq.Workplane:
    """Generate a 3D extension spring with hooks.

    Extension springs have zero pitch in the body
    (coils touch) and hooks at each end.

    Args:
        d: Wire diameter (mm)
        D: Mean coil diameter (mm)
        N_a: Number of active body coils
        hook_length: Length of straight hook section (mm).
                     Defaults to D (one coil diameter).

    Returns:
        CadQuery solid of the spring
    """
    R = D / 2
    if hook_length is None:
        hook_length = D

    # Body: tightly wound coils (pitch = wire diameter)
    body_pitch = d  # Coils touching
    body_length = N_a * body_pitch

    points_per_coil = 16
    total_body_points = int(N_a * points_per_coil)

    # Build full path: bottom hook + body + top hook
    all_points = []

    # Bottom hook: half-loop + straight section
    hook_points = 8  # Half a coil
    for i in range(hook_points + 1):
        angle = np.pi * (i / hook_points)  # 0 to pi
        # Hook curves from center out to coil radius
        hx = R * np.sin(angle)
        hy = -R * np.cos(angle)  # Start pointing down
        hz = -hook_length * (1 - i / hook_points)
        all_points.append((hx, hy, hz))

    # Body coils (start where the bottom hook ends: at angle pi/2;
    # i starts at 1 so that this point is not added twice)
    for i in range(1, total_body_points + 1):
        angle = np.pi / 2 + 2 * np.pi * (i / points_per_coil)
        t = i / points_per_coil
        x = R * np.cos(angle)
        y = R * np.sin(angle)
        z = t * body_pitch
        all_points.append((x, y, z))

    # Top hook: half-loop + straight section
    z_top = body_length
    start_angle = np.pi / 2 + 2 * np.pi * N_a
    for i in range(1, hook_points + 1):
        angle = start_angle + np.pi * (i / hook_points)
        hx = R * np.cos(angle)
        hy = R * np.sin(angle)
        hz = z_top + hook_length * (i / hook_points)
        all_points.append((hx, hy, hz))

    # Create path and sweep
    spring = sweep_round_wire(all_points, d)
    return spring
```

```python
import cadquery as cq
import numpy as np

def torsion_spring(
    d: float,          # Wire diameter (mm)
    D: float,          # Mean coil diameter (mm)
    N_a: float,        # Number of active coils
    leg_length: float, # Length of straight legs (mm)
    leg_angle: float = 90.0,  # Angle between legs (degrees)
) -> cq.Workplane:
    """Generate a 3D torsion spring with straight legs.

    Torsion springs have zero pitch (coils touching)
    with two straight legs that extend tangentially.

    Args:
        d: Wire diameter (mm)
        D: Mean coil diameter (mm)
        N_a: Number of body coils
        leg_length: Length of each straight leg (mm)
        leg_angle: Angle between the two legs (degrees)

    Returns:
        CadQuery solid of the spring
    """
    R = D / 2
    body_pitch = d  # Coils touching

    # The second leg leaves the coil along its tangent. A whole number
    # of coils would put the two legs 180 degrees apart, so the body
    # gets a part of a turn extra to reach the requested leg angle.
    body_turns = N_a + (180.0 - leg_angle) / 360.0

    points_per_coil = 16
    total_body_points = int(round(body_turns * points_per_coil))

    all_points = []

    # First leg: straight section tangent to the coil
    # Extends tangentially from the start of the coil
    n_leg_pts = 4
    start_angle = 0
    # Tangent direction at start: perpendicular to radius
    tx = -np.sin(start_angle)
    ty = np.cos(start_angle)
    for i in range(n_leg_pts, 0, -1):
        frac = i / n_leg_pts
        # The leg lies behind the start of the coil (minus sign),
        # so the wire runs straight into the first coil
        lx = R * np.cos(start_angle) - tx * leg_length * frac
        ly = R * np.sin(start_angle) - ty * leg_length * frac
        lz = 0
        all_points.append((lx, ly, lz))

    # Body coils
    for i in range(total_body_points + 1):
        angle = start_angle + 2 * np.pi * (i / points_per_coil)
        t = i / points_per_coil
        x = R * np.cos(angle)
        y = R * np.sin(angle)
        z = t * body_pitch
        all_points.append((x, y, z))

    # Second leg: extends tangentially at the end
    end_angle = start_angle + 2 * np.pi * total_body_points / points_per_coil
    # The extra part of a turn above sets the angle between the legs
    leg2_angle = end_angle
    tx2 = -np.sin(leg2_angle)
    ty2 = np.cos(leg2_angle)
    z_end = (total_body_points / points_per_coil) * body_pitch
    for i in range(1, n_leg_pts + 1):
        frac = i / n_leg_pts
        lx = R * np.cos(leg2_angle) + tx2 * leg_length * frac
        ly = R * np.sin(leg2_angle) + ty2 * leg_length * frac
        lz = z_end
        all_points.append((lx, ly, lz))

    # Create path and sweep
    spring = sweep_round_wire(all_points, d)
    return spring

# Torsion spring: 1.2mm wire, 10mm coil, 5 coils, 25mm legs
torsion = torsion_spring(
    d=1.2, D=10.0, N_a=5, leg_length=25.0, leg_angle=90.0
)
cq.exporters.export(torsion, "torsion_spring.step")
```

## Complete Design Workflow: Compression Spring from Requirements

<InArticleAd />


### Design Requirements

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Maximum load $F_{\max}$ | 80 N |
| Minimum load $F_{\min}$ (installed) | 20 N |
| Working deflection $\delta_w$ | 15 mm |
| Maximum outer diameter | 22 mm |
| Material | Music wire (ASTM A228) |
| Life requirement | Infinite life ($> 10^6$ cycles) |
| End type | Squared and ground |

### Step-by-Step Design Calculation

<Steps>
1. **Determine spring rate from load and deflection requirements**

2. **Select wire diameter and compute coil geometry**

3. **Verify static stress with Wahl correction**

4. **Verify fatigue life with Goodman analysis**

5. **Compute all physical dimensions and generate 3D model**
</Steps>

The script needs a rate of $k = (80 - 20) / 15 = 4$ N/mm, fixes the spring index at 8 and tries five wire sizes. The coil diameter follows from the index and the coil count from the rate, so the stress checks decide. Wire of 1.6 mm fails both checks (754 MPa against 715 allowed, $n_f = 1.26$). Wire of 1.8 mm passes with little margin ($n_f = 1.56$). Wire of 2.0 mm passes well (482 MPa against 692 allowed, $n_f = 1.90$) and is chosen. The final spring has 10 active coils, a rate of 3.99 N/mm, a solid height of 24.0 mm and a free length of 46.3 mm.

```python
import numpy as np

# === DESIGN REQUIREMENTS ===
F_max = 80.0     # N, maximum working load
F_min = 20.0     # N, minimum load (installed)
delta_w = 15.0   # mm, working deflection (F_max - F_min)
OD_max = 22.0    # mm, maximum outer diameter
material = "music_wire"
G = 81_700.0     # MPa, shear modulus (music wire)
n_s = 1.3        # Static safety factor
n_f = 1.5        # Fatigue safety factor

# === STEP 1: Spring Rate ===
k = (F_max - F_min) / delta_w  # N/mm
print(f"Required spring rate: k = {k:.2f} N/mm")

# === STEP 2: Wire Diameter and Coil Geometry ===
# Start with a trial spring index C = 8 (good starting point)
C_trial = 8.0

# Iterate: select wire diameter, compute coil geometry
# The design equation for wire diameter from stress limit:
# d >= (8 * K_w * F_max * C / (pi * tau_allow))^(1/3)

# Trial wire diameters
for d_trial in [1.6, 1.8, 2.0, 2.2, 2.5]:
    D = C_trial * d_trial
    OD = D + d_trial

    if OD > OD_max:
        continue

    # Number of active coils
    N_a = G * d_trial**4 / (8 * D**3 * k)

    if N_a < 3 or N_a > 20:
        continue

    # Free length (squared-and-ground ends)
    # Solid height
    L_s = (N_a + 2) * d_trial
    # Deflection at F_max
    delta_max = F_max / k
    # Clash allowance (15% of working deflection)
    delta_clash = 0.15 * delta_w
    L_f = L_s + delta_max + delta_clash

    # Check stress
    Kw = wahl_factor(C_trial)
    tau_max = Kw * 8 * F_max * D / (np.pi * d_trial**3)
    S_ut = music_wire_tensile_strength(d_trial)
    S_sy = 0.45 * S_ut
    tau_allow = S_sy / n_s

    # Check fatigue
    fatigue = goodman_fatigue_check(
        F_min, F_max, D, d_trial, S_ut,
        shot_peened=False, n_f_required=n_f
    )

    print(f"\n--- Trial: d = {d_trial} mm ---")
    print(f"  D = {D:.1f} mm, OD = {OD:.1f} mm")
    print(f"  N_a = {N_a:.1f} coils")
    print(f"  L_f = {L_f:.1f} mm, L_s = {L_s:.1f} mm")
    print(f"  tau_max = {tau_max:.1f} MPa")
    print(f"  tau_allow = {tau_allow:.1f} MPa")
    print(f"  Static OK: {tau_max < tau_allow}")
    print(f"  S_ut = {S_ut:.0f} MPa")
    print(f"  Fatigue n_f = {fatigue['n_f']:.2f}")
    print(f"  Infinite life: {fatigue['infinite_life']}")

# === STEP 3: Select Best Design ===
# Choose d = 2.0 mm based on results above
d = 2.0                          # mm (standard music wire)
D = C_trial * d                  # 16.0 mm mean coil diameter
OD = D + d                       # 18.0 mm outer diameter
ID = D - d                       # 14.0 mm inner diameter
N_a = G * d**4 / (8 * D**3 * k)  # Active coils
N_a = round(N_a)                 # Round to nearest integer

# Recalculate actual spring rate with rounded N_a
k_actual = G * d**4 / (8 * D**3 * N_a)
print(f"\n=== FINAL DESIGN ===")
print(f"Wire diameter:     d = {d} mm")
print(f"Mean coil diameter: D = {D} mm")
print(f"Outer diameter:    OD = {OD} mm")
print(f"Inner diameter:    ID = {ID} mm")
print(f"Active coils:      N_a = {N_a}")
print(f"Spring rate:       k = {k_actual:.3f} N/mm")

# Physical dimensions
L_s = (N_a + 2) * d
delta_max = F_max / k_actual
delta_clash = 0.15 * (F_max - F_min) / k_actual
L_f = L_s + delta_max + delta_clash
pitch = (L_f - 2 * d) / N_a

print(f"Solid height:      L_s = {L_s:.1f} mm")
print(f"Free length:       L_f = {L_f:.1f} mm")
print(f"Pitch:             p = {pitch:.2f} mm")
print(f"Total coils:       N_t = {N_a + 2}")

# === STEP 4: Final Verification ===
Kw = wahl_factor(D / d)
tau_max = Kw * 8 * F_max * D / (np.pi * d**3)
S_ut = music_wire_tensile_strength(d)
S_sy = 0.45 * S_ut
n_static = S_sy / tau_max

fatigue_final = goodman_fatigue_check(
    F_min, F_max, D, d, S_ut,
    shot_peened=False, n_f_required=n_f
)

print(f"\n=== STRESS VERIFICATION ===")
print(f"Wahl factor:       K_w = {Kw:.3f}")
print(f"Max shear stress:  tau = {tau_max:.1f} MPa")
print(f"Shear yield:       S_sy = {S_sy:.0f} MPa")
print(f"Static safety:     n_s = {n_static:.2f}")
print(f"Fatigue safety:    n_f = {fatigue_final['n_f']:.2f}")
print(f"Infinite life:     {fatigue_final['infinite_life']}")
```

## Extension Spring: Initial Tension

<InArticleAd />


The coils of an extension spring are wound pressed together. The initial tension $F_i$ (N) is the pull needed before they start to separate, so the force at a deflection $\delta$ is:

$$
F = F_i + k \cdot \delta
$$

The maker sets $F_i$ by twisting the wire as it is coiled. It is specified by the shear stress it causes:

$$
\tau_i = \frac{8 F_i D}{\pi d^3}
$$

A practical $\tau_i$ is 10 to 30% of the shear yield strength, lower at a high spring index $C$.

The function sizes the body as for a compression spring, then checks the hook. A hook is a sharp bend that carries the full load in bending, so `sigma_hook` is usually the highest stress and the hook is where extension springs break.

```python
def extension_spring_design(
    F_max: float,       # Maximum load (N)
    F_i: float,         # Initial tension (N)
    delta_max: float,   # Maximum deflection (mm)
    D: float,           # Mean coil diameter (mm)
    d: float,           # Wire diameter (mm)
    G: float = 81_700,  # Shear modulus (MPa)
) -> dict:
    """Design an extension spring with initial tension.

    Returns dictionary of design parameters.
    """
    # Spring rate (not counting initial tension)
    k = (F_max - F_i) / delta_max

    # Active coils
    N_a = G * d**4 / (8 * D**3 * k)

    # Body length (coils touching)
    L_body = (N_a + 1) * d  # +1 for end coil

    # Hook stress concentration factor (typical)
    # Hooks have higher stress than the body
    K_hook = (4 * (D/d)**2 - (D/d) - 1) / (4 * (D/d) * ((D/d) - 1))

    # Maximum body stress
    C = D / d
    Kw = wahl_factor(C)
    tau_body = Kw * 8 * F_max * D / (np.pi * d**3)

    # Hook bending stress (the critical point)
    sigma_hook = K_hook * 16 * F_max * D / (np.pi * d**3)

    return {
        'k_N_per_mm': k,
        'N_a': N_a,
        'L_body_mm': L_body,
        'tau_body_MPa': tau_body,
        'sigma_hook_MPa': sigma_hook,
        'K_hook': K_hook,
    }
```

## Torsion Spring Stress Analysis

<InArticleAd />


A torque on the legs winds the coil tighter, and that bends the wire along its whole length. Bending stress pulls one side of the wire and presses the other. It is highest on the inside of the coil:

$$
\sigma_b = K_b \cdot \frac{32 M}{\pi d^3}
$$

where $\sigma_b$ is in MPa, $M$ is the applied torque (N·mm), and $K_b$ corrects for the curvature of the wire, as $K_w$ did for shear:

$$
K_b = \frac{4C^2 - C - 1}{4C(C - 1)}
$$

The angular rate is the torque for each radian of rotation $\theta$:

$$
k_\theta = \frac{M}{\theta} = \frac{E d^4}{64 D N_a} \quad \text{(N-mm per radian)}
$$

where $E$ is Young's modulus (MPa), the stiffness in bending. It replaces $G$ because the wire is bent, not twisted.

:::caution[Torsion Spring Direction]
Load a torsion spring only in the direction that tightens the coil. Loading it open puts tension on the inside surface and can break it early. State the winding direction (left-hand or right-hand) on the drawing.
:::

:::tip[Instrument it]
The real rate of a spring often differs from the calculated one, because wire diameter varies in manufacture and a spring relaxes over time. Press it against a load cell and read the travel with a dial gauge to get the true force-deflection curve. An ESP32 or STM32 can log it. On a spring that holds a preload, send the force over MQTT to a broker you run, or to [siliconwit.io](https://siliconwit.io), our monitoring service, to keep the record that shows relaxation over thousands of cycles; it works with any MQTT client.
:::
